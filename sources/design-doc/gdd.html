Rock Paper Scissors Fighter

Two fighters, three moves, five seconds to choose. Attack beats Throw, Throw beats Block, Block beats Attack, and each win hits for a different amount. The cabinet is playable: it boots through the logo intro and the home menu into a best-of-three match against the random computer.

  • Buttons: click or tap Attack, Throw, Block, or press 1 2 3.
  • Menus: joystick (drag it) or arrow keys move the cursor, any button or Enter confirms, menu items can be tapped, Esc goes back. Click the cabinet first to give it the keyboard.

01

Overview

A browser fighting game built on rock paper scissors, drawn as an upright arcade machine. Version 1 is single player against a random computer. Online play and an Android app come later, and they shape every technical choice now.

Simple to read

Three moves, one clear winner each exchange, no hidden mechanics.

Satisfying reveal

Both picks lock in, the move icons drop in, and the fighters act it out with flash, shake and sound.

Built for online

The rules never trust the client, and the menus already have a slot for multiplayer.

Platforms: desktop and mobile browsers first (the same vertical cabinet on both), then Android by wrapping the same build.

02

Core rules

Winning moveBeatsDamage
ThrowBlock20
AttackThrow15
Block counterAttack10
  • A tie deals no damage. Only the loser of an exchange takes damage, so there is no double knockout.
  • Each fighter starts a round with 100 health, a value to tune in playtests.
  • A round ends at 0 health. A match is best of three rounds, and health resets each round.
  • The computer picks one of the three moves at random, with equal odds.
03

The mind game

Because the three wins hit for different amounts, this is not a coin flip. Throw is the tempting big hit, and that temptation is what players read and punish.

  1. Throw pays the most, so players lean toward it.
  2. Expecting a Throw, the opponent picks Attack.
  3. Expecting that Attack, you switch to Block.
  4. Blocking too often gets punished by Throw. Repeat.

The balanced mix

When both players play perfectly, these are the shares of picks where neither has an edge. Change the damage values to see how the mix moves.

Attack
44%
Block
33%
Throw
22%

Rule of thumb: each move's share is proportional to the damage of the move it beats. Attack beats the 20 damage Throw, so Attack is picked most. Throw beats the 10 damage Block, so Throw, the biggest hit, is picked least.

Health changes the stakes. With 15 health left, an Attack or Throw win ends the round but a 10 damage Block counter does not. Late-round picks feel different from early ones.

Against the random computer, Attack and Throw each gain about 1.7 net damage per exchange on average, while Block loses about 3.3. A computer that plays the balanced mix, or punishes patterns, would make a stronger opponent later.

Menus and screen flow

NEW

The game starts with a logo intro, then the home screen, where Start game begins a match against the CPU. After the match, Retry plays again and Main menu goes home. This small flow is the frame the multiplayer menus plug into later.

  • Controls in menus: the joystick or arrow keys move the cursor, any of the three buttons (or Enter, Space, a tap on the item) confirms, Esc or Android back goes back. In a fight the joystick stays decoration.
  • Built to grow: every menu is a list of items drawn by one menu component, so online play, settings, pause or a how-to-play screen is a new item plus its screen.
  • Retry starts a new match with the same setup. Online, it becomes a rematch request that both players must accept.
  • Pixel perfect: fades step through four palette levels instead of a smooth blend, and menu text uses a 5 × 7 pixel font.
05

Exchange flow

Every exchange runs the same way: a 5 second countdown, a simultaneous reveal, then the animation and damage.

  1. PickA 5 second countdown starts (3 seconds is the alternative to playtest). Press Attack, Throw or Block on the cabinet, or 1, 2, 3. You can change your pick until the timer ends; only you see it.
  2. Lock inAt 0, anyone without a pick gets a random move. Picks stay hidden until both are locked.
  3. RevealEach fighter's move coin drops in above their head with a bounce, at the same moment for both.
  4. Act it outBoth fighters play their move. The winner lands with a hit flash, a hit sound and a small screen shake. On a tie the moves clash and nobody takes damage.
  5. ResolveThe loser's health bar drains by the damage amount.
  6. NextNext countdown, or a knockout: a "Round 2" or "Final round" banner starts the next round, or the match ends on the result screen with Retry and Main menu.

In online play the timer and the random fallback pick run on the server, so both players see the same reveal at the same time.

06

The arcade cabinet

The whole game is an upright arcade machine, so the layout is vertical and works the same in a desktop browser and on a phone held upright. In the browser the cabinet stands in a dark arcade room; on a phone it fills the screen with the buttons in thumb reach.

07

Art direction

The fighters are martial artists in gi, drawn short, wide and cute with a slight Japanese chibi feel: white gi with a red headband against blue gi with a gold band. The tone is an '80s neon arcade: synthwave sunset, neon grid floor, a neon torii. Sprites are 64 pixels tall.

Still open. The overall look is parked to revisit later, and the real fighter art is not settled. The free packs checked lack block and throw frames, so the plan is better paid packs or AI-generated fighters (PixelLab is the best fit found) cleaned up into sprite sheets. Everything on this page is a placeholder drawn in code.

Animations each fighter needs: idle, attack, throw, block (with counter strike), hurt, KO and a win pose.

08

Pixel perfect and polished

Pixel perfect rules

  • One pixel size for everything: fighters, cabinet, HUD, icons, effects.
  • Whole-number scaling only (2×, 3×, 4×), nearest neighbour; leftover space is arcade room.
  • Everything snaps to whole pixels, including camera, shake and tweens.
  • No free rotation or scaling of sprites; turns and squash are drawn as frames.
  • Pixel UI: a pixel font at native size, bars and glows drawn as pixels.
  • One limited palette shared by all art.

Game feel

  • Screen shake that is short, punchy and scaled to damage.
  • Hit flash, sparks, impact frames, dust and a brief hit-pause on big hits.
  • Lively animation with anticipation, overshoot and follow-through; idle is never still.
  • A sound for every action, layered and varied.
  • Buttons press down, bars drain smoothly, banners slam in.
  • A dedicated tuning pass before release, with every value in one tuning file.
09

Audio

Sound effects

  • Countdown ticks for the last 3 seconds and a lock-in sound at the reveal.
  • A different hit per winning move and a clash on a tie.
  • KO, "Round 1, fight", a win jingle, and menu cursor and confirm blips.
  • Source: 37 hits/punches, CC0.

Music

  • A menu loop on Home and Result, a fight loop in the match, with mute and separate volumes.
  • To start: a CC0 chiptune loop.
  • Later: music composed by Claude as code (Tone.js) or as MIDI rendered to audio.
